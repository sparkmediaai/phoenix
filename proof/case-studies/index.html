Case study one

The glass cab operating panel: $1,000 to $350 at 2,500 units.

The problem

A residential elevator manufacturer, a Phoenix customer for close to twenty years, needed a new cab operating panel for its next cab: all glass, an integrated display, and a phone mounted on the back because the code requires one. Revision one came in near $1,000 a unit. The customer said it was too expensive, and it was.

What was built

Instead of losing the job, Phoenix took the target back to the factory: $350 to $400 a unit at a first quantity of 2,500. The part was redesigned for that machine and that price rather than pulled from a shelf. Same function, same glass front, same phone, on its second revision now.

The result

$350 per unit at 2,500 units, against $1,000 for revision one

One approved part, carried across the customer's cab lineup. That is what a design win looks like from the builder's side of the table.
